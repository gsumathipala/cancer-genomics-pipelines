One row per specimen. Each row becomes its own run, with its own patient details, its own assay and its own report — which is the difference between this and the old batch tick-box, where every sample inherited one patient record.

Runs execute one at a time, in worksheet order. Analysis and reporting finish for one sample before the next begins; that is the job queue's normal behaviour, not something you need to manage.

1 · Fill the worksheet from a folder

Point at the sequencing run's FASTQ folder and every sample found becomes a row, with its file paths already filled in. You then say who each one belongs to — the part only you know.

{% if scanned %}Scanned {{ scanned }} — {{ rows|length }} sample(s) found.{% endif %}

Scanning replaces the rows below, so scan first and fill in details afterwards. You can also skip this and type rows by hand.

2 · Settings shared by every row

Tumour site, specimen type and referring clinician here are defaults: they fill any row that leaves the matching column blank, because a worksheet is often one tumour type arriving from one clinician, and setting that once beats setting it twelve times. Every one of them can be overridden per row — which matters most for the site, since PCGR tiers actionability against it and the same variant can be predictive in one tissue and merely oncogenic in another. Resources left blank (COSMIC, dbSNP, gnomAD, the PCGR bundle) are filled from what is installed, exactly as on the single-sample forms.

3 · The samples

Untick a row to leave it out without deleting it. Rows with no sample name are ignored.
